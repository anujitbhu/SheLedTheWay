# 2026-11-24 | Oprah Winfrey — Research

## Selected episode (research, not script)

In 1976, 22-year-old Oprah Winfrey arrived at Baltimore’s WJZ-TV to co-anchor the six o’clock news.[1][6][7]
After fewer than eight months, WJZ removed her from the evening newscast and assigned her to morning cut-ins; she later recalled feeling that she had failed at news and was about to be fired.[1][3][6]
In 1978, Winfrey co-hosted WJZ’s local talk show *People Are Talking*, a format she later described as feeling like home.[1][3][7]
In 1984, she moved to Chicago to host *A.M. Chicago*.[2][3][5]
The program was renamed *The Oprah Winfrey Show* in 1985 and entered national syndication in 1986.[2][3][4]

## Claims for a future script

- In 1976, Winfrey was 22 and joined Baltimore station WJZ-TV as a co-anchor of its six o’clock news.[1][6][7]
- On April 1, 1977, after about eight months, she was reassigned from the evening newscast to morning cut-ins; sources describe a reassignment, not a firing from WJZ.[1][3][6]
- Winfrey recalled that she was moved to cut-ins while earning $22,000 a year; retain the amount as a historical figure only, with no inflation conversion or unsupported claim about motive.[1][3][6]
- Her on-record explanation for accepting the talk-show role was: “I only came to co-host a talk show because I had failed at news and I was going to be fired.”[3]
- *People Are Talking* was a Baltimore talk show she co-hosted beginning in 1978; the precise first-show date (August 14, 1978) is reported by Baltimore Magazine, while CBS Baltimore corroborates the year.[1][7]
- Winfrey moved to Chicago in 1984 to host *A.M. Chicago*.[2][3][5]
- The Chicago program was renamed *The Oprah Winfrey Show* in 1985 and entered national syndication in 1986.[2][3][4]

## Open questions

- The exact *People Are Talking* first-show date, August 14, 1978, appears in Baltimore Magazine; CBS Baltimore confirms the year but not that day. Use 1978 unless another independent source confirms the exact date.[1][7]
- CBS Baltimore dates Winfrey’s departure from Baltimore to December 16, 1983, while the official biography and Illinois Broadcasters Association place her Chicago hosting role in 1984. Distinguish departure from the *A.M. Chicago* start.[2][5][7]
- Keep “taken off the evening news” from implying that she was fired: the sources describe reassignment to early-morning cut-ins.[1][3][6]
- “Built a media empire” is broad. For this carousel, the verified arc is the 1984 Chicago hosting role, the 1985 rename, and 1986 national syndication; avoid implying the demotion alone caused later success.[2][3][4]

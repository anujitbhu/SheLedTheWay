# 2026-11-25 | Anandibai Joshi | Perseverance | Reel

## Episode
Anandibai Joshi lost her infant son after childbirth; she believed better medical care could have changed the outcome.[1][2]
She saw women’s medical care as a need she could help meet.[1][2]
In 1883, she left India to study at the Woman’s Medical College of Pennsylvania in Philadelphia.[1][2]
She earned her medical degree in 1886, becoming the first woman from India to earn a degree in Western medicine in the United States.[1][2]
She returned with a position awaiting her at Albert Edward Hospital in Kolhapur, but became ill before she could begin practice; she died on February 26, 1887.[1][2]

## Claims
- Her infant son died after childbirth, and she connected the loss to the need for better medical care.[1][2]
- In 1883, she left India and enrolled at the Woman’s Medical College of Pennsylvania in Philadelphia.[1][2]
- She graduated in 1886 with a medical degree; the Library of Congress describes her as the first woman from India to earn a degree in Western medicine in the United States.[1][2]
- The government of Kolhapur intended to appoint her to a medical role at Albert Edward Hospital; she became seriously ill before practice and died on February 26, 1887.[1][2]

## Open questions
- The Library of Congress and Drexel use different qualified descriptions of her “first”; use the United States degree qualification and avoid the broader label “first Indian woman doctor.”[1][2]
- Drexel dates her sailing home to October 1886, while the Library of Congress dates her arrival in India to November; they mark different points in the journey, so do not treat them as conflicting.[1][2]
- The precise age of her infant son and the ten-day interval are stated by Drexel but not corroborated in the Library of Congress account; leave them out of the script.[1][2]

## On-screen hook
IBM fired Lynn Conway in 1968. She later helped change how engineers designed chips.

## Voiceover
Yorktown Heights, 1968. Lynn Conway tells IBM she is transitioning; the company fires her. Starting over under a new identity, she rebuilds an engineering career while fearing that discovery could cost it again. At Xerox PARC, she works with Carver Mead to create scalable rules for designing VLSI chips. In 1978, Conway teaches the first VLSI design course at MIT; their textbook carries the method into classrooms. The shared process helps engineers design complex chips more efficiently. The work outlives the door IBM closed. She led the way.
